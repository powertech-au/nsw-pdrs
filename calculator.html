    Behind the numbers

    What the calculator is doing

    Certificates are deemed, not measured. The Rule takes a notional peak contribution from the battery, stretches it over the six-hour peak window and the equipment lifetime, then converts the result into certificates at one per 0.1 kW.

    Questions

    Things that catch projects out

    Why does my inverter size change the answer?

    Counted Battery Capacity is the lesser of the Usable Battery Capacity and four hours multiplied by the battery inverter output. An undersized inverter quietly caps the certificate count no matter how much storage you install.

    Full value needs an inverter of at least Usable ÷ 4. Separately, the usable capacity must not exceed six times the inverter output or the battery is not eligible at all, which sets a hard floor of Usable ÷ 6. Anything above Usable ÷ 4 earns nothing extra under the scheme, so oversized inverters have to justify themselves on demand-charge reduction instead.

    Do I have to install solar?

    Not for the business activities. Solar is what unlocks the higher rate, not what makes you eligible. Under BESS4 and BESS5 the difference is 0.10 against 0.067 kW/kWh, so roughly a third of the value turns on it.

    If you do claim the higher rate, the new solar must be at least a quarter of the usable battery capacity and the battery must go in within 90 days of it, before or after. BESS1 is different: it requires solar already at the same NMI.

    Why is the discount capped on some jobs?

    BESS1, BESS3 and BESS4 all carry a minimum customer payment: $200, $1,000 and $5,000 excluding GST respectively. It is measured as the Net Amount actually paid across the whole job after every discount, and it is binary. Fall a dollar below it and the implementation cannot use Equation 2b at all, so the claim produces no certificates rather than fewer.

    On smaller jobs with generous certificate prices this can bind before you expect it, so the calculator flags the headroom. Non-cash inducements and in-kind payments never count towards the minimum.

    Can I claim the federal battery rebate as well?

    Up to a point. The Cheaper Home Batteries Program covers systems between 5 kWh and 100 kWh of nominal capacity, so a battery above 100 kWh nominal is outside the program altogether rather than merely capped, and earns no battery STCs at all.

    Within that window, certificates are created only for the first 50 kWh of usable capacity, and since 1 May 2026 that allowance is tapered: full rate to 14 kWh, 60% from 14 kWh to 28 kWh, and 15% from 28 kWh to 50 kWh. Any battery over 50 kWh usable therefore lands on the same STC number.

    Is BESS1 actually open?

    Only narrowly. BESS1 has been closed to new implementations since 1 July 2025 under clause 6.10.1. From 1 July 2026 clause 6.10.2 reopens it, but only for work delivered at a Government Owned and Managed Site or through an initiative that was an Exempt Energy Program at the implementation date. An ordinary residential BESS1 job cannot create certificates.

    When can I actually claim?

    BESS3, BESS4 and BESS5 commence on 1 September 2026, and only implementations completed on or after that date can create certificates. The Implementation Date is the day installation of all end-user equipment is finished, so a job that straddles the date is governed by when it finishes, not when it was quoted or ordered.
